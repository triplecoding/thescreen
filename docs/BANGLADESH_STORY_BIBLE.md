# Bangladeshi Story Craft — The Screen story bible

Use this every time you write or fix a story for **The Screen** (দ্য স্ক্রিন · গল্পে দেখি জীবন) or any Bangla
life-story video. The goal: a viewer in Mirpur, Sylhet, Khulna or a Dubai labour camp watches to the last second and
thinks *"এটা তো আমাদের ঘরের গল্প"*.

Format rules for The Screen live in `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/STORY_GUIDE.md` (box "UPDATE 5 Nov 2026") and
`docs/SCRIPT_FORMAT.md`. This bible is about **what makes the story true, Bangladeshi and impossible to skip**.

---

## 1. The non-negotiables (owner rules)

1. **Story only.** No book card, no lesson list, no "কী শিখলাম", no "পর্দাটা তোমার দিকে", no explaining voice-over.
   Never name the book, author or theory. The book idea is only the hidden spine.
2. **Ending = the story's own after-scene + one 2-line closing** (`outro`: line1 "…", line2). Nothing after it.
3. **2:00–2:30** — ≈ 1,450–1,560 narration characters, 26–32 beats, 8–12 dialogue lines, 2–3 speaking characters.
4. **Fully original.** Never reuse a plot from the reference videos or `data/used.json`.
5. **Realistic.** No fairy tales, no sudden millionaires, no villains twirling moustaches. Small true changes.
6. Bangla script only (the voice is Bangla only); natural English loanwords are written in Bangla script.

## 2. Bangladeshi human psychology — the forces that move our people

Build every story on one of these inner forces. Show it through behaviour, never name it.

| Force | What it looks like on screen | Story fuel |
|---|---|---|
| **লোকে কী বলবে** (social face) | hides failure from relatives, fake "সব ঠিক আছে", wedding show-off | the cost of pretending; the relief of truth |
| **মান-সম্মান / ইজ্জত** | won't ask for help, won't take back a word, father refuses a favour | pride vs need; dignity kept in a humble way |
| **লজ্জা** | can't say "না", can't ask for salary, girl can't speak about harassment | the first time someone finally speaks |
| **অভিমান** (hurt pride with someone you love) | silent treatment, food left uneaten, not picking up calls | one small act that melts it — never a big speech |
| **মায়া** | keeps an old cycle, feeds the stray dog, sends money though broke | attachment that looks foolish until we learn why |
| **ত্যাগ** (parents' quiet sacrifice) | mother eats last, father walks to save bus fare, elder sister quits study | the child discovers it late — the turn |
| **দায়িত্ব** (duty to family) | eldest son carries the whole family, probashi father, garments sister | duty vs own dream; finding a third way |
| **কৃতজ্ঞতা / ঋণ** | never forgets who helped in the flood, repays a teacher 20 years later | kindness returning in an unexpected form |
| **তুলনা** (comparison) | "পাশের বাড়ির ছেলে", cousin's BCS, neighbour's flat | chasing someone else's life; choosing your own |
| **ভয় আর নিরাপত্তা** | gold earrings instead of bank, rice drums, government job obsession | old wounds behind odd habits (flood of '88, '98, 2004) |
| **চাপা কষ্ট** (unspoken pain) | men don't cry, mothers "ঠিক আছি", child hides bullying | the moment it is finally said — 2 lines, not a monologue |
| **গোষ্ঠী / একসাথে** | neighbours in a flood, hawkers together, mohalla committee | many small people together change one big thing |

**Hierarchy is real:** elders get আপনি; parents to children তুই/তুমি; friends তুই; colleagues আপনি → তুমি;
a younger person never says "তুমি ভুল" to an elder — they ask a question, bring proof, or act. Use this: the
"turn" is often a younger person finding a respectful way to change an elder's mind.

**Love is rarely said.** Fathers show love by providing; mothers by food; siblings by teasing and covering for each
other. The most powerful lines are indirect: *"খেয়েছিস?"*, *"ভাড়াটা আছে তো?"*, *"রাতে একবার ফোন দিস।"*

## 3. Emotional engines that keep Bangladeshi viewers watching

Pick one engine per story; mix with one force from §2.

1. **The hidden sacrifice revealed** — a passbook, a receipt, an old letter, a medicine strip shows what a parent gave up.
2. **অভিমান broken by a small act** — a favourite dish, a call at the right hour, one honest sentence.
3. **Dignity of the small worker** — rickshaw puller, guard, tea-seller, garments worker, cook — quietly right.
4. **Honesty under pressure** — wrong bKash transfer, found wallet, exam paper leak; honesty that costs, then returns.
5. **Truth vs show** — the expensive phone/wedding/flat vs what people actually remember.
6. **The question that changes everything** — a child or friend asks one simple question; the adult goes silent.
7. **Injustice → quiet, clever justice** — no violence, no revenge; proof, unity, patience, a written rule.
8. **Return** — going back to the village, to the mother, to an old teacher, to one's first craft.
9. **Generations** — grandmother's old habit makes sense; grandson teaches her bKash; both learn.
10. **Probashi** — Dubai/Malaysia/Saudi father, video calls, missed weddings, money vs presence.

**Avoid overused bait:** a death in every story, cancer twists, "and then he became a millionaire". One in ten
stories may touch illness or loss — handled with restraint.

## 4. Realism bank — specific beats real life

Use exact, believable details. Specific > general, always.

**Money (2025–26, BDT, approximate):** garments operator 12,500–16,000/month · entry office job 18,000–30,000 ·
private-school teacher 10,000–20,000 · tuition 2,000–5,000 per student · rickshaw 700–1,200/day · CNG fare 150–400 ·
mess seat 3,000–5,000 · tea 10–15 · singara 10 · bus 20–40 · BCS coaching 15,000–40,000 · bKash cash-out ~1.85%.

**Places:** Mirpur 10, Jatrabari, Mohammadpur, Uttara, Old Dhaka lanes, Gazipur & Ashulia garments, Narayanganj,
Chattogram port, Sylhet tea gardens, Khulna, Barishal launch ghat, Rangpur, Rajshahi, Cox's Bazar, haor, char,
upazila health complex, union parishad, primary school, madrasa (no religious debate), coaching centre, mess, hostel.

**Life texture:** load-shedding, Dhaka traffic, Friday bazar, Eid salami, Eid travel home, chandraat tailor rush,
Pohela Boishakh, winter pitha, monsoon floods, power-cut exams, school tiffin, Facebook groups, imo calls with
probashi, NGO kisti, samity schemes, "Hello, আপনার বিকাশে ভুল করে টাকা গেছে" scams, gold savings, rice drums,
mobile recharge shops, tea-stall adda, cricket in the lane, wedding gate, gaye holud, hospital corridors at night.

**Objects that carry emotion:** passbook, envelope marked "কোনো কিছুর জন্য না", tiffin carrier, old cycle, umbrella,
sewing machine, school ID card, first salary sweets, broken phone, prescription, Eid panjabi, rice drum, tea flask.

## 5. Language — how people really talk

- **Narrator:** clean চলিত Bangla, short sentences, present-feeling past. ≤ 90 characters per line.
- **Dialogue:** spoken, imperfect, warm. Contractions and fillers are fine: *আরে, শোন, দাঁড়া, হুম, আচ্ছা, থাক*.
- **Natural mixed language** (write English words in Bangla script): অফিস, বস, ফাইল, মিটিং, সরি, প্লিজ, থ্যাংকস,
  টেনশন, প্রেশার, ডিল, ওকে, ম্যাডাম, স্যার, প্রমোশন, ইন্টারভিউ, রিপোর্ট, সিলেবাস, কোচিং, বিকাশ, রিচার্জ.
  Keep it to what that character would say — a village grandmother says almost none; a Dhaka office worker says many.
- **Regional flavour — a touch, never a wall:** খাইছি, যামু, কইলাম, পাইছি, হইছে (rural/Dhaka), সিলেটি/চাটগাঁইয়া
  only a word or two so every viewer still understands.
- **Address terms set the relationship instantly:** আব্বা/আব্বু/বাবা · আম্মা/আম্মু/মা · ভাইয়া/আপু · চাচা/মামা/খালা/
  ফুপু · ভাবি · দাদু/নানু · স্যার/ম্যাডাম · ওস্তাদ · মামা (to a rickshaw puller/tea-seller).
- **Voice engine tips (ElevenLabs Bangla):** write numbers as Bangla words or digits, never Latin abbreviations;
  use "—" and "…" for pauses; avoid long English phrases; one emotion per line; put the key word in `*stars*`.

## 6. The story-only structure (2:00–2:30)

| Time | Part | Beats | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | **Hook** | 1–2 | A number + a stalemate: "চার বছর, তিনবার বিসিএস, খরচ তিন লাখ।" Show the widget. |
| 5–30 s | **World & want** | 4–6 | Who, where, what they want, who opposes. 2–3 dialogue lines. A specific object. |
| 30–45 s | **What they tried** | 1–2 | `list` card ✕ ✕ ✕ — the obvious ways all failed. |
| 45–60 s | **Low point** | 2–3 | Night, silence, a missed call, food left uneaten. `pause` 0.7–1.0. |
| 60–95 s | **The turn** | 5–8 | One small, humane, specific act — a question, a written page, a visit, listening. Back-and-forth dialogue; the other person's silence. |
| 95–125 s | **After-scene** | 4–6 | Show the change in life: a call, a meal, a returning customer, a child laughing. One line of dialogue that lands. |
| 125–140 s | **Payoff + closing** | 2 | One glow line (optional, story-language), then `outro`: two lines. |

**Retention rules:** something changes every 15–20 seconds (new place, new person, new information, a reversal);
never two narrator beats in a row longer than 15 seconds without a face or a widget; the after-scene must be
*shown*, not summarised.

**2-line closing formulas** (outro): contrast or image, never a lecture.
- *"অতীতের খরচ ফেরত আসে না… / ভবিষ্যৎটা এখনো খরচ হয়নি।"*
- *"রাগ সিদ্ধান্ত নেয় এক মিনিটে… / দাম দিতে হয় অনেক বছর।"*
- *"নজর দিয়ে মানুষ আটকানো যায়… / ভরসা দিয়ে কাছে রাখা যায়।"*
Pattern: line1 = the common belief or the visible thing (ends with "…"); line2 = the deeper truth, 3–6 words.

## 7. Dialogue craft

- 8–12 dialogue lines; each ≤ 90 characters; one idea per line.
- **Subtext beats statement:** not "আমি তোমাকে ভালোবাসি, আম্মা" but "আম্মা, এই মাসে গাভী বেচতে হবে না।"
- Let people disagree for real; the opponent must have a fair reason (fear, pride, tradition, money).
- The wise line comes from an unexpected mouth: a child, a guard, a grandmother — and it is a **question**,
  not advice. Advice-speeches kill retention.
- End scenes on a line or a look, not on narration that explains the line.

## 9. Never

Real public figures, brands, political parties or current politics, religious debate, violence or gore, self-harm,
romance manipulation, medical instructions, mocking any region/group/accent, defamation, copied book text, fake
statistics presented as research, fairy-tale riches, cruelty as the "turn", children in danger for shock value.
Religion may appear as life (Eid, a prayer mat in the corner, puja holiday) — never as argument.

## 10. Titles, thumbnails, captions (SEO for Bangladeshi viewers)

- **YouTube title:** emotional story hook ≤ 80 chars + one emoji, numbers help: *"চার বছর, তিন লাখ টাকা— বন্ধুর একটা প্রশ্ন
  বদলে দিল রানার পথ ☕"*. No "| Book". The pipeline adds "| বাংলা গল্প" and credits the book in the description.
- **Description line 1:** the hook in 1–2 sentences ending with "…" (shown in search).
- **Tags:** story theme in Bangla + Banglish (বাবার গল্প, মায়ের গল্প, bangla golpo, jiboner golpo, motivational
  story bangla), relationship, setting.
- **Thumbnail text:** 2–3 bold words that create a question: *"শুধু আজকের দিন"*, *"কোনো কিছুর জন্য না"*.
- **Facebook caption:** payoff line + emoji → a question about the viewer's own life + 👇 → no links, no book line.
  Questions that get comments: *"তোমার জীবনের সেই মানুষটা কে…?"*, *"শেষ কবে…?"*, *"তুমি হলে কী করতে?"*

## 11. Self-check before you save a script (score each 1–5; rewrite anything below 4)

1. **Hook:** would a scrolling thumb stop in 2 seconds?
2. **Truth:** could this happen on a real Dhaka/village street this year? Are the numbers right?
3. **Force:** which Bangladeshi inner force (§2) drives it — is it shown, not told?
4. **Turn:** is the turning act small, humane and specific — not a speech?
5. **Dialogue:** do 2–3 people really talk, with subtext and the right address terms?
6. **After-scene:** do we *see* life after the change?
7. **Closing:** exactly two short lines, an image or contrast, no lesson?
8. **Clean:** no book/theory named, no "কী শিখলাম", nothing from the Never list?
9. **Length:** `python pipeline/validate.py` estimates 128–145 s?

## 12. Original premise seeds (use, combine, never copy existing scripts)

1. A Gazipur garments worker hides her overtime from her mother because the mother would stop eating meat to "save".
2. A launch-ghat porter keeps the lost school ID cards of children for years; one comes back as an engineer.
3. A probashi father learns to cook his daughter's favourite bhorta over video call for her birthday.
4. A mess cook in Nilkhet secretly reduces his price for one student who always eats only dal.
5. A retired postman reads letters to illiterate mothers in a Facebook era — until a son finally calls.
6. A Chattogram rickshaw puller saves for his son's laptop coin by coin; the son saves for the father's cataract.
7. A grandmother refuses the gas stove — the clay chulha was the only thing left from the '98 flood.
8. A Sylhet tea-garden girl's school essay "আমার বাবা" is read on the school mike; the father is outside holding the gate.
9. An office peon's son tops the board exam; the colleagues who ignored the peon don't know what to say.
10. Two Eid-panjabi tailors compete on chandraat; one stays open all night for the other's unfinished orders.
11. A widow runs a tiny phone-recharge shop; a bKash scam call targets her and her customers save her.
12. A father keeps failing the driving test for a CNG licence; his daughter becomes his teacher.
13. A Rangpur farmer's son sells online — the father refuses until a buyer's video thanks "চাচার আলু".
14. A girl's first salary goes to the auntie who paid her SSC form fee secretly years ago.
15. A Barishal launch cabin boy is blamed for a missing bag; an old passenger's honesty saves him.
16. A Dhaka landlord's mother quietly lowers the rent of a student who carries her groceries every Friday.
17. A school guard who never studied memorises every student's name; on his retirement the gate is full.
18. A brother pretends to "not like" mangoes for 15 years so his sister gets his share; at her wedding she sends a basket.
19. A coaching-centre owner realises his best student studies under the street light due to load-shedding.
20. A village health worker convinces mothers to vaccinate by visiting at the pond ghat, not with a mike.
